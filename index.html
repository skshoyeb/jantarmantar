At a glance

3,000–7,000+

Detained in Delhi today (media)

Context & sources

The Hindu (6:53 PM) restates that over 5,000 protesters were detained in Delhi, including CJP's Abhijeet Dipke, CPI(M) leaders and student activists. Delhi Police said at 6:53 PM they have begun releasing detainees (a senior officer via The Hindu); no count of releases given.

NDTV (7:41 PM, with PTI inputs): over 1,500 detained across Delhi; many were released later in the evening. Scroll (8:16 PM): police reportedly detained more than 7,000 protesters in Delhi. NDTV (~10 PM): at least 3,000 detained across Delhi; most released by night.

Delhi Police sources via ANI (9:38 PM): all detainees released from detention centres except CJP leaders, for whom due legal process will be followed. As of 10:05 PM. ThePrint (5:28 PM): around 3,500 detained in Delhi; police say detentions could last 'up to 24 hours'.

Officials told PTI at 4:53 PM that Delhi Police detained over 3,000 people during the protest (The Hindu's summary says 3,300; The Tribune says 3,000). A Delhi Police officer told The Hindu around 5,000 were detained till 2 PM. TMC MP Abhishek Banerjee claimed (ANI, ~5:07 PM) about 2,500 were detained.

A Delhi Police officer told The Hindu around 5,000 protesters were detained across Delhi till 2 PM and taken to temporary detention facilities (The Hindu live blog, 2:57 PM entry; Scroll repeated it at ~4:45 PM). Earlier figures: TOI 1,500+ (midday); PTI citing police sources ~1,000 (3:57 PM).

Outside Delhi: Chennai 100+ (NDTV, ~4:22 PM); Bengaluru at least 50 AISA students at Freedom Park (The Hindu, 3:21 PM); Mumbai: over 400 detained so far at August Kranti Maidan (Mumbai Police via ANI, 5:39 PM;

PTI had ~200 at 5:18 PM); actor Imran Khan and his relative Zayn Marie Khan were among those detained (NDTV, 8:30 PM); Pune: about 80–100 detained at Lal Mahal Chowk and taken to Lashkar police station (IANS, 8:46 PM); earlier Scroll reported detentions with no number. HT (10:47 PM): police say nearly 4,000 detained.

As of 11:10 PM. NDTV (12:12 AM headline): at least 3,000 were detained in Delhi and all of them were released except the CJP leaders and Dhruv Rathee. NDTV also reports at least 25,000 police and paramilitary personnel were deployed. As of 12:15 AM.

A new habeas corpus petition was filed in the Supreme Court by CJP coverage coordinator Gaurav Bharti (about 11:39 PM to 12:10 AM). It seeks production and release of Abhijeet Dipke, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka and others, their whereabouts, the legal grounds for each detention, and preservation of CCTV, police diaries and movement registers. Background

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), a youth movement, along with Left groups, student unions and civil-society groups, called a protest at Jantar Mantar, New Delhi, on Sat 10 Oct 2026. They want Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar to resign and the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of voter rolls scrapped. Critics say SIR has removed more than 130 million names (NPR/AP). Delhi Police refused permission and locked down central Delhi: BNSS Sec 163 orders, about 45 metro stations shut until gates reopened around 10:36 PM, trains cancelled, internet cut within 4 km, and roughly 23,000 CAPF personnel deployed. Police detained CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke and leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka on board their flight. YouTuber Dhruv Rathee was detained at his Gurugram home. Opposition leaders, lawyers and journalists were also detained. INDIA bloc street protests have been running since about 2 Oct, and Rahul Gandhi was detained on 6 and 7 Oct.

Legal context (reference)

  • Article 22(1): anyone arrested has the right to be told the grounds and to consult a lawyer. Article 22(2): they must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours.
  • D.K. Basu guidelines (SC, 1997): arrest memo, informing a relative, and the place of custody must be disclosed.
  • BNSS Sec 163: prohibitory orders (formerly CrPC 144). BNSS Sec 172: police may detain people who resist lawful directions.
  • Note: these are general legal standards shown for reference. Whether they were broken in any specific case has not been decided by a court.
